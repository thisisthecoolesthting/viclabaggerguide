## Tires, Windshields, and Getting Fitment Right on a Bagger

Tires and windshields are the two touring components most directly tied to how a bagger actually rides at highway speed, and mirror adjustment is the free fix most riders never bother to get right. Here's what determines tire life, which windshield brands actually reduce buffeting, and how to set up mirrors so they cover your blind spot instead of duplicating what you can already see.

### Tires: mileage depends on the tire, not a fixed number

When to change Harley touring tires? Most last 8,000-15,000 miles depending on riding style and brand, but the real signal is tread depth and cracking, not the odometer. Between changes, a dedicated cleaner like [White Lines WL-32 Whitewall & Tire Cleaner](/products/b0dvrg6ft4-white-lines-wl-32-street-juice-products-whitewall-tire-clean/) keeps whitewalls from staining while you're doing your tread checks anyway. What are the best motorcycle tires for touring? Dunlop Elite, Michelin Commander, and Metzeler ME 888/Cruisetec cover most of what serious touring riders choose between, trading off tread life against wet-weather grip slightly differently depending on the line.

### Windshields: height and shape beat brand loyalty

Klock Werks and Memphis Shades come up most often for baggers specifically, each offering multiple heights so you can dial in wind protection against visibility rather than accepting a one-size shield. What actually cuts helmet buffeting at highway speed is the windshield's height and curvature relative to your seating position -- a shield that's perfect for one rider's height can be too short or too tall for another on the identical bike.

### Mirrors: adjust for blind spot, not for the lane you're already watching

How do I adjust the side view mirrors? Position each one so you see a sliver of your own shoulder at the inside edge -- this covers the blind spot instead of duplicating your direct rearward view, which is the setup most riders default to without realizing it maximizes blind spots on either side.
