If a bike genuinely sat for two years, change the oil before riding it regardless of the previous change date."

    answer: "AGM remains the more practical default for most riders -- it's compatible with any standard charging system, tolerates a wider range of charger types, and costs less. Lithium batteries are significantly lighter (often half the weight) and hold a charge longer in storage, but they require a charging system and voltage regulator that's compatible with lithium's different charging profile, which isn't guaranteed on older bikes without verification."
  - question: "What are the three oils to change on a Harley?"
    answer: "Engine oil, primary chaincase oil, and transmission oil are the three separate reservoirs on most Harley Touring models -- unlike a car, these don't share a single oil supply, so each needs its own fluid and its own change interval. Mixing up fluid types (using engine oil where primary or transmission fluid is specified) is a common mistake since Harley's factory-recommended weights and formulations differ between the three."
  - question: "What is the 12 second rule for motorcycles?"
    answer: "The 12-second rule is a hazard-scanning guideline from motorcycle safety courses: continuously scan the road 12 seconds ahead of your current position (roughly a city block at typical speeds) to spot developing hazards -- a car about to pull out, a pothole, gravel -- with enough time to react smoothly rather than braking or swerving abruptly. It's a scanning-distance rule, distinct from the 4-second rule, which covers following distance to the vehicle directly ahead."
  - question: "What is the 4 second rule on a motorcycle?"
    answer: "The 4-second rule is a following-distance guideline: pick a fixed point on the road (a sign, a shadow, a pavement mark) and make sure at least 4 seconds pass between the vehicle ahead of you passing that point and you reaching it. It gives more stopping and reaction margin than the 2-second car-following rule because motorcycles are more affected by road surface issues and less visible to the drivers around them."

## Oil, Battery, and the Safety Rules Every Rider Learns Eventually

The mechanical questions here -- oil filters, battery specs, change intervals -- are straightforward once you know Harley Touring engines run three separate oil systems instead of one, and that battery choice comes down to AGM's compatibility versus lithium's weight savings. The two "second rule" questions are actually two different safety guidelines from motorcycle safety courses, not two versions of the same rule, which is a common point of confusion.

### Three oils, three intervals

What are the three oils to change on a Harley? Engine oil, primary chaincase oil, and transmission oil -- three separate reservoirs, each needing its own fluid rather than sharing supply the way a car's engine and transmission sometimes do partially overlap. How often do you change the oil in a Harley 103? Engine oil every 5,000 miles or annually, whichever comes first; primary and transmission fluid stretch longer, typically 10,000-20,000 miles depending on model year.

### Battery: AGM for compatibility, lithium for weight

Is AGM or lithium better for Harley-Davidson? AGM is the safer default -- compatible with any standard charging system and cheaper up front. Lithium cuts weight significantly and lasts longer in service (often 8-10 years versus AGM's 3-5), but only delivers on that if your charging system and voltage regulator are confirmed compatible with lithium's different charging profile first.

### The two "second rules" aren't the same rule

What is the 4 second rule on a motorcycle? A following-distance guideline -- pick a fixed point on the road and make sure 4 seconds pass between the vehicle ahead reaching it and you reaching it. What is the 12 second rule for motorcycles? A separate hazard-scanning guideline -- continuously look 12 seconds ahead of your current position to spot developing hazards with enough time to react smoothly. One covers the gap behind the car ahead of you; the other covers how far down the road you should be actively watching.
